The gap

Across UK testbeds, campus deployments and regional programmes, equipment performs and coverage maps look convincing. Yet twelve months later, the work is still stuck in pilot mode.

Funded for capital build, not operational use

Grant funding pays for masts, gateways and hardware integration, while training, support and year-three operating budgets sit outside anyone’s remit.

Our role: build commercial and governance pathways before procurement opens, so the service has a clear operational owner from day one.

Measured by throughput instead of human outcomes

Technical trials celebrate latency and peak speed, yet frontline staff cannot see what becomes faster, safer or easier on Monday morning.

Our role: anchor every trial to a plain-language operational problem agreed by both leadership and the people doing the work.

Public ambition and private investment out of step

Local authorities buy inclusion and place-based value; network operators invest against predictable demand. Meeting only at formal tender locks both sides into defensive positions.

Our role: run structured pre-procurement workshops that align anchor demand, risk sharing and realistic delivery models.

How we work

We do not sell hardware or run indefinite discovery. Every engagement moves through four concrete stages with named outputs.

  1. 1

    Assess

    Map the connectivity landscape, regulatory constraints and stakeholder incentives shaping what is genuinely viable.

    Deliverable: Evidence brief and barrier map

  2. 2

    Frame

    Bring technical teams, policy leads and end users into the same room to agree which problem is worth solving first.

    Deliverable: Shared priority and test criteria

  3. 3

    Enable

    Design the commercial model, partner alignment and operational support required for confident adoption.

    Deliverable: Adoption roadmap and partner plan

  4. 4

    Measure

    Establish plain-language outcome measures so sponsors have the evidence to scale, adapt or stop.

    Deliverable: Impact framework and scale decision

Start with the problem, not the network.

A useful first conversation does not require a finished specification. Three plain questions are enough to establish whether we can help:

  • What should better connectivity make easier for your organisation or community?
  • Who needs to trust and use it for the investment to count?
  • What evidence would convince you it is working?
